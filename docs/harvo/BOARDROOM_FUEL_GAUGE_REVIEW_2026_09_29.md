## Business question and verdict

A host funding four campaigns needs to know, for each flight, what Encho has charged, what media spend the networks have *reported*, when that report was current, what the network has confirmed about pause/delivery, what amount has been independently settled, and which action is safe. A moving percentage alone answers none of the last four questions.

**Verdict:** The main v2 Campaign Studio has a useful, relatively honest read-only media-usage meter and separate host pause/cancel/refund requests. It is not a complete fuel-and-money-control system; there is no canonical safe top-up journey. The Antigravity candidate's “Reactor Core” should not be merged into v2 as written. It combines captured host charges with media spend, treats missing spend as zero, invents runway from a planning budget, can label an approved campaign live, duplicates the existing meter, and offers Refuel through a retired API. Its visual design can be salvaged after the evidence contract is corrected. Calling it production-ready would materially mislead hosts.

## The money model the UI must teach

1. **Host charge/capture** includes only amounts in the accepted itemized financial quote. Never call it “media fuel.” Markup/tax/fees are separate, and the exact current policy must be resolved from an accepted contract.
2. **Authorized media allocation** is a flight/revision-bound maximum Encho has authorized under finance and the provider plan. It is not a refund promise or necessarily the provider's current effective limit.
3. **Provider-reported spend** is a delayed, dated observation in provider currency and account time zone. Reported zero is valid only if a compatible successful report explicitly says zero; no report means **unknown**.
4. **Unbilled or delayed exposure** remains possible after a pause request and even after confirmed pause. Do not infer final unspent cash from allocation minus a delayed report.
5. **Refundable amount** comes only from canonical finance/settlement state after obligations and variance are accounted for. It is distinct from the animated bar.

For a compatible, version-bound report, the visual may show `reported utilization = provider-reported media spend / confirmed media allocation` (bounded display at 100%, with explicit overage). “Remaining allowance” is at best provisional; do not label it available cash. If any input is absent, stale, incompatible, or in reconciliation, show *report unavailable/stale* rather than a green 100% gauge. Keep integer minor units on the server and format for display only.

Google's official budget model distinguishes average daily and campaign total budgets; average daily spending can vary by day and total budgets have different provider constraints. Thus planned daily budget is not observed burn rate, and a generic “days of fuel left” promise is unsound without a campaign-type-specific model and uncertainty. References: [Google Ads API campaign budget overview](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/campaigns/budgets/overview?hl=en), [Google Ads spending limits](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637?hl=en).

## Recommended host experience (proposal, not approval)

- **Campaign card:** channel, offer, desired state, last confirmed provider state/time, media-plan utilization with source/as-of, and an explicit “report delayed” or “reconciliation needed” state. Four flights remain separate; aggregate totals must deduplicate shared outcomes and never transfer money between flights.
- **Campaign detail:** one clear visual meter, then four independent financial rows: accepted host charge, authorized media, reported media spend and finance-confirmed refundable amount. Show report window and pending provider operation in plain language.
- **Pause:** “Request pause” records an idempotent command and estimated exposure warning. Show `requested → sent → confirmed paused` or `unknown outcome → reconciliation`, with alerts and escalation. A request never immediately renders the network paused or releases funds.
- **Stop/close:** distinguish pre-provider cancellation from post-provider stop/settlement. No automatic resume when calendar availability returns. Later invoices/corrections remain visible.
- **Top-up:** do not expose an active Refuel button until the canonical journey exists. A future action should present an incremental itemized quote, explicit acceptance, verified capture, atomic journal/reservation/risk gate, idempotent provider budget amendment on the exact bound flight, authenticated readback, and an applied/failed/unknown result. Payment success alone must not show “campaign refueled.” Never silently activate a paused campaign. Provider capability may make a particular budget change unavailable; offer a safe replacement flight rather than pretending all budget types mutate alike.
- **Advice:** a low percentage should invite a performance check, not automatically urge purchase. Show qualified inquiries, reconciled bookings, occupancy, spend pace and uncertainty; avoid unsupported ROI, artificial urgency, animations on every poll, and “100% genuine” claims. Respect reduced motion and readable colors/text.

## Founder refinement — three flights, channel choice and continuing a winner

The founder clarified the intended experience: each of three simultaneous campaigns has its own visible media-spend gauge; a host can fund and pause each independently; a host may choose how much of a combined marketing intention goes to Google versus Meta; a successful campaign should be extendable with an additional itemized payment without rebuilding its creative and targeting. This is product intent, **not** an approved wallet, fee/tax contract, provider mutation or launch decision.

**Product judgment:** This is strategically good. The differentiator is a host-controlled, legible acquisition portfolio, not an animated meter. It is dangerous only if “add money” means an unallocated platform wallet or a UI increment that outruns the provider/ledger. A campaign that has merely burned through budget is not necessarily a winner. Recommend funding prompts based on qualified inquiries, attributable fulfilled bookings, margin/inventory and evidence confidence; show insufficient data honestly.

### What the source supports and what it does not

- `components/marketing/types.ts` models each `StudioCampaign` and `CampaignDraft` with exactly one provider (`GOOGLE` or `META`), one campaign identity/revision and one media plan. `src/migrations/010_harvo_marketing_workflow.sql` enforces the same provider choice. Multiple independent campaigns already fit the schema and workspace projection, though a dedicated three-flight visual portfolio and a safe top-up do not.
- `src/lib/marketing/financeQuote.ts` already preserves separate `mediaMinor.META` and `mediaMinor.GOOGLE` amounts in its quote contract. This is a useful accounting primitive; it does **not** turn one workflow campaign into a two-provider campaign or authorize moving reserved funds between campaigns.
- Both provider adapters expose `updateBudget` with recorded-budget checks, durable provider-operation claims and readback. Meta's present compiler uses an ad-set lifetime budget. Google's adapter distinguishes daily from campaign-total budget and rejects a shared budget. This is partial provider plumbing; the v2 workflow has no host incremental-quote/capture/amendment command or budget-update job. `financeBridge.authorize` still fences spending to the original revision's media allocation.
- Official Google documentation distinguishes daily from total campaign budgets, and a total campaign budget cannot simply be assumed to behave like a daily one. Any continuation must be capability-tested for the exact campaign, budget resource and flight dates: [Google Ads API budgets](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/campaigns/budgets/overview?hl=en), [Google Ads spending limits](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637?hl=en).

### Recommended product and finance structure

One **host marketing plan** may be a user-facing grouping, but each **provider flight** is its own campaign, quote allocation, reservation, external identity, metrics stream, pause state and settlement. Example: a host intends ₹10,000 in media and chooses ₹6,000 Meta / ₹4,000 Google *before* accepting the itemized charge. The host sees two gauges and two pause controls under one plan summary. Encho fees and applicable taxes are itemized in the accepted quote(s) on top of or within that media choice according to approved policy; they are never silently counted as ad spend. A third flight has a third independent gauge. Aggregate outcomes must deduplicate a booking influenced by multiple flights.

For the first safe release, recommend **two provider-specific quotes/flight reservations and one visual plan summary** rather than retrofitting a single financial reservation to mean two spendable accounts. A later single checkout may group the transactions only after atomic allocation, partial-failure and refund semantics are designed. Do not create a fungible “fuel wallet” as a shortcut. If the host asks to shift ₹1,000 from Meta to Google after acceptance, that is a new bounded financial/provider amendment, not a slider-only edit or automatic transfer. Default: no transfer.

“Continue this campaign” should be a host-facing *intent*, not a guarantee that the same external campaign object can always be reused. If exact current content, offer/inventory, provider account, policy and budget type remain valid, attach an immutable incremental funding amendment and read back the updated provider budget on the same external object. If the flight ended, provider budget/date change is unsupported, material facts changed, or provider status is ambiguous, create a **successor flight in the same visible campaign series** after fresh review. Preserve old spend, quote and performance evidence separately. A payment never auto-resumes a paused flight.

### Host controls and acceptance sequence

1. **Per-flight read view:** Media authorized, provider-reported spend, report source/window/as-of, provisional unspent allocation, separately settled/refundable funds, desired and confirmed provider state. Three simultaneous cards and the plan summary must reconcile exactly without double counting.
   The current client polls the workspace every 30 seconds (`components/marketing/api.ts`), while the observation scheduler runs every five minutes (`src/server/marketing/worker.ts`); network reporting can lag further. Therefore label this **latest available reported spend**, not a live account balance or second-by-second burn.
2. **Pause/stop:** Host submits an idempotent request. Show queued, provider confirmed, or reconciliation-required; keep reporting late spend and show when finance closes exposure. Resume requires explicit host intent and current inventory/funding/account/policy checks.
3. **Add budget:** Host chooses the target flight and amount; server returns a new itemized incremental quote with media, Encho charge, tax and total. Host accepts and pays. Only after verified capture and atomic journal/reservation/risk authorization may the worker request the provider budget change; only after readback should the UI say “additional budget applied.” On failure or unknown outcome, retain funds/evidence in a reconciliable state and offer support, never duplicate the mutation or claim success.
4. **Distribution choice:** Let hosts choose or accept an evidence-labeled suggested Meta/Google allocation before paying. Set hard minimums/exposure caps, supported currency/account checks and a confirmation screen. Administrative strategy still owns unsafe provider settings. Later allocation changes require an explicit amendment and independent settlement.
5. **Winner guidance:** Do not equate low fuel, high CTR or high spend with success. Show inquiry quality, fulfilled-booking evidence, attributable contribution where available, inventory and uncertainty. A low budget on an underperforming flight should suggest pause or review; a winning flight can offer a transparent continuation quote.

**Failure probes before release:** duplicate payment click and cross-process replay; lost payment/COMMIT response; simultaneous top-ups on the same flight; provider accepted write with lost response; budget-resource drift; delayed spend after pause; Google shared-budget rejection; Meta ad-set budget readback mismatch; ended flight; price/availability change; host switching accounts; one provider succeeding while the other fails; chargeback, refund and late invoice. Verify tenant RLS and host/Admin roles on every route and worker effect. These tests are proposed acceptance criteria, not currently passing evidence.

## Follow-up boardroom question — independent pause, fixed channel amounts and master-account billing failure

**Source answer:** A host can currently request pause on one provider-specific campaign without requesting pause on the other campaigns. `CampaignStudio.tsx` sends `/campaigns/:id/pause`; `router.ts` returns 202 and `workflow.ts` locks, checks and queues the named campaign and revision. This is a requested pause, not proof of an immediate remote stop. The provider worker/readback and late-spend reconciliation determine the eventual confirmed state. The list of three campaigns is independent, although the main list cards do not yet display three simultaneous gauges.

At creation the host currently selects either Meta or Google and enters that one flight's fixed media plan. The other channel requires its own campaign. The current UI cannot accept one combined amount and atomically split it across both networks. A finance quote type containing `mediaMinor.META` and `mediaMinor.GOOGLE` is not proof of a delivered dual-channel checkout. A future visual plan may group two separately funded provider flights, preserve separate limits and offer an explicit pre-quote allocation choice; post-acceptance transfer is a new financial amendment, not a UI slider.

**Payment-failure answer:** The host is **not guaranteed** to see a Meta master-account billing error today. `MetaAdProvider.account()` checks identity, active account status and currency and collapses mismatches to `META_ACCOUNT_UNAVAILABLE`; authoritative campaign readback can turn that into generic `UNKNOWN`/reconciliation-required. The host projection and `DeliveryEvidence` use generic network-attention language; they have no verified billing-incident field. Provider `checkHealth()` exists but was not found wired into a persistent operational monitor in the inspected server/marketing paths. The gauge reads Meta Insights `spend` or Google `metrics.cost_micros`, with delayed observation; these are provider-reported campaign costs, **not** the master account's payment success, invoice settlement, or a real-time billing balance. A flat or missing spend report cannot identify why delivery stopped. Google documents that its programmable billing workflow depends on monthly invoicing; API billing coverage must be established for the actual Encho account configuration before promising native billing parity: [Google billing overview](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/billing/overview).

**Proposed upgrade:** Introduce an independently observed, account-scoped incident stream with provider/account identity, supported status evidence, source/as-of, confidence, incident lifecycle and admin audit. Classify `BILLING_CONFIRMED` only from an authenticated provider signal or verified operator evidence; account unavailable alone is `CAUSE_UNKNOWN`. Fan out a sanitized host banner only to affected flights, for example “Meta delivery is interrupted; Encho is resolving an account issue. Your last reported spend is ₹X as of [time]. No action is required from you.” Do not expose Encho's master billing details or solicit a host top-up to fix Encho's payment problem. Gate new activation/spend on confirmed account health; keep ambiguous remote outcomes in reconciliation and alert operations. Show the fuel meter's dated reported spend separately from provider delivery, account incident, pending exposure and finance-confirmed refundable amount. If a provider is down, do not label the campaign paused or its unspent media automatically refundable. Each provider flight retains its own host pause action and settled money history. This remains a design proposal, not an implemented control or approved financial policy.
